## 📌 Executive Summary

This portfolio project demonstrates the end-to-end management of a **hospital patient scheduling system implementation** designed to reduce scheduling delays, prevent booking conflicts, improve provider and room utilization, standardize appointment workflows, strengthen patient communication, and improve operational visibility.

As the simulated **Healthcare Systems Implementation Project Manager**, I managed the initiative across business case development, project initiation, current-state workflow analysis, stakeholder engagement, requirements management, scheduling-rule design, Agile delivery, solution validation, privacy and security planning, testing, organizational change, training, go-live readiness, cutover, hypercare, KPI tracking, benefits realization, operational handoff, and formal closure.

The project demonstrates the coordination required across hospital operations, scheduling teams, clinicians, IT, security/privacy, QA, analytics, and executive leadership during a healthcare technology implementation.

> **Portfolio Disclosure:** This is a simulated professional portfolio project. Hospital names, patient volumes, schedules, budgets, stakeholders, operational metrics, financial figures, and outcomes are scenario-based and are presented to demonstrate practical project-management and healthcare IT implementation capabilities.

## 🏢 Business Problem

The simulated hospital relied on fragmented scheduling processes across phone calls, spreadsheets, email, department-specific calendars, and manual coordination.

This created several operational challenges:

- Long scheduling times
- Provider double-booking risk
- Room and equipment conflicts
- Inconsistent appointment durations
- Limited visibility into provider capacity
- Limited visibility into room utilization
- Manual cancellations and rescheduling
- Inconsistent waitlist management
- High no-show rates
- Inconsistent urgent appointment handling
- Delayed patient confirmations
- Weak reporting
- Poor cross-department coordination
- Limited auditability

Leadership approved a centralized scheduling initiative to improve operational efficiency and patient access.

```text
Urgent Request
      ↓

      ↓
Reserved Urgent Slot Checked
      ↓
Approved Override if Needed
      ↓
Appointment Scheduled
```

## ⚙️ Scheduling Business Rules

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Provider Hours** | Appointments must fall within approved provider availability |
| **Appointment Duration** | Duration determined by appointment type |
| **Room Requirement** | Required room/resource must be available |
| **Specialty Restriction** | Specialty visits require eligible providers |
| **Double-Booking** | Blocked unless approved override exists |
| **Buffer Time** | Applied when clinically or operationally required |
| **Urgent Slots** | Reserved capacity maintained for priority requests |
| **Waitlist** | Prioritized by urgency and request date |
| **Same-Day Scheduling** | Permitted only when validated capacity exists |
| **Cancellation** | Cancellation reason captured |
| **No-Show** | Recorded for follow-up and reporting |
| **Provider Unavailable** | Affected appointments routed to rescheduling workflow |
| **Room Unavailable** | Booking prevented or alternate resource identified |

## 🔙 Rollback Strategy

Rollback would be considered if:

- Critical scheduling functionality failed
- Widespread access failure occurred
- Unsafe double-booking behavior appeared
- Sev 1 defect occurred
- Provider availability became unreliable
- Room/resource scheduling became corrupted

Rollback activities included:

1. Pause new bookings
2. Activate approved downtime process
3. Notify users and leadership
4. Revert affected configuration
5. Restore validated scheduling information
6. Retest critical workflows
7. Conduct revised Go/No-Go review

## 💡 Key Lessons Learned

1. Scheduling rules must be documented before configuration begins.
2. Provider and room availability should be treated as linked constraints.
3. Appointment duration standards have a major impact on capacity planning.
4. Clinician and scheduling-team involvement should begin early.
5. Testing must include exception workflows, not just successful bookings.
6. Privacy and access requirements should be integrated into design and testing.
7. Training and change management directly affect adoption.
8. Go-live criteria should be measurable and agreed upon before deployment.
9. Hypercare is essential for identifying operational issues quickly.
10. Scheduling improvement should be measured through both patient-access and operational KPIs.
