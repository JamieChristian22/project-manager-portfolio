# Evidence-led interview walkthrough

## Five-minute demonstration

**First minute:** State that this is a portfolio simulation. Explain the business constraint, your designed role, and the acceptance condition. Avoid presenting fictional budgets as money you managed for an employer.

**Second minute:** Show the project-specific options and decision authority. Explain why the preferred option is conditional, what information could change it, and who approves the consequence.

**Third minute:** Open the linked issue, action, decision, test and budget records. Show the unresolved dependency and the named owner. Explain why the gate is on hold despite some completed tasks.

**Fourth minute:** Run the validator. Increase a critical task duration in a disposable copy and show the new finish. Explain actual plus remaining cost, authorized changes, and negative variance.

**Fifth minute:** Discuss the handoff and benefits measurement. Identify one limitation of the original evidence and explain what you would collect in a real project to resolve it.

## Follow-up questions to prepare

| Question | Evidence to use |
|---|---|
| What do you do when an owner misses a commitment? | Preserve due date, record blocker, agree recovery, escalate based on dependency |
| How do you prevent scope creep? | Change impact, approval authority and baseline update after authorization |
| How do you know a status is reliable? | Source population, reporting date, evidence reference and exception review |
| Why not release when most tests pass? | Mandatory failed/not-run checks and explicit residual-risk authority |
| How does a coordinator differ from a PM? | Coordinator tracks and escalates; authorized PM/sponsor makes scope and release decisions |
| How do you avoid overstating results? | Distinguish designed scenario, calculated metric and independently observed outcome |
| What would you improve next? | Resolve documented source discrepancies and validate the native tools |

Use the project documents as evidence, not a memorized claim of experience. Do not describe supplemental exercise records as original project execution logs.
